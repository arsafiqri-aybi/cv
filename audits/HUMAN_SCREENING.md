# Human Screening Audit v1

## Goal

Evaluate whether a human screener can quickly and correctly interpret the most relevant evidence.

## Audit dimensions

### 1. Relevance visibility
- Are the strongest role-relevant signals easy to find?
- Are low-value details displacing high-value evidence?

### 2. Clarity
- Are contributions understandable without hidden context?
- Are acronyms or internal names explained when needed?

### 3. Attribution
- Is individual contribution distinguishable from team/company outcomes?
- Are leadership claims proportional to actual ownership?

### 4. Credibility
- Are claims specific enough to be believable?
- Are metrics interpretable?
- Do statements create suspicious precision or exaggerated causality?

### 5. Cognitive load
- Is information density manageable?
- Are sentences/bullets overloaded?
- Does section structure reduce search effort?

### 6. Inference risk
Check for likely reviewer over-inference from:
- titles;
- prestigious names;
- formatting polish;
- gaps;
- unusual chronology;
- demographic cues;
- personality-like adjectives.

### 7. Fit perception
Distinguish:
- evidence-supported requirement match;
- likely recruiter P–J fit perception;
- vague P–O/culture-fit interpretation.

Do not optimize for cultural mimicry.

### 8. First-pass test
Without relying on a mythical universal time limit, ask:
- Can the target role be inferred?
- Can top 3–5 relevant evidence items be found quickly?
- Are chronology and section purpose obvious?
- Is there any immediate contradiction?

## Critical failures

- material fact is ambiguous or misleading;
- relevant evidence is buried by low-value content;
- ownership inflation;
- unexplained metric;
- illegible/overloaded layout;
- sensitive personal information with no justified need;
- generic summary that displaces evidence;
- persuasive adjective unsupported by evidence.
