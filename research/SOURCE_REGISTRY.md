# Initial Source Registry

This is a starter map, not a saturated literature review.

## S01 — Current state of personnel selection science

**Sackett, Lievens, & Landers (2026). _Hiring People in Organizations: The State and Future of the Science_. Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior, 13, 49–75.**

DOI: 10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-020924-072127

Use:
- current personnel-selection umbrella;
- validity;
- fairness/bias;
- technology and AI;
- applicant reactions;
- changing assessment methods.

Evidence class: high-quality integrative review.

## S02 — Recruitment as distinct scientific domain

**Breaugh (2013). _Employee Recruitment_. Annual Review of Psychology, 64, 389–416.**

DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143757

Use:
- recruitment perspective;
- applicant-side processes;
- recruitment methods/messages and prehire outcomes.

Evidence class: review.

## S03 — Recruitment + selection + technology synthesis

**Potočnik, Anderson, Born, Kleinmann, & Nikolaou (2021). _Paving the way for research in recruitment and selection: Recent developments, challenges and future opportunities_.**

Use:
- demonstrates recruitment, selection, and new technology as related but distinct research areas;
- supports broader umbrella than selection alone.

Evidence class: review of meta-analyses / reviews.

## S05 — Selection as signaling

**Bangerter, Roulin, & König (2012). _Personnel selection as a signaling game_. Journal of Applied Psychology, 97(4), 719–738.**

DOI: 10.1037/a0026078

Use:
- information asymmetry;
- applicant and organization signaling;
- strategic self-presentation;
- faking / adaptive signaling dynamics.

Evidence class: theory/integrative article.

## S06 — Resume content → perceived fit → hiring recommendation

**Tsai, Chi, Huang, & Hsu (2011). _The Effects of Applicant Résumé Contents on Recruiters' Hiring Recommendations: The Mediating Roles of Recruiter Fit Perceptions_. Applied Psychology, 60(2), 231–254.**

DOI: 10.1111/j.1464-0597.2010.00434.x

Use:
- evidence that recruiters use resume information to form fit perceptions;
- fit perceptions can mediate hiring recommendations.

Boundary:
- field study in campus recruiting in Taiwan;
- hiring recommendation is not job-performance validity.

Evidence class: field study.

## S07 — Writing / composition and job-search outcomes

**Wingate, Robie, Powell, & Bourdage (2025). _The Signals That Matter: Resumes, Cover Letters, and Success on the Job Search_. International Journal of Selection and Assessment, 33(3), e70022.**

DOI: 10.1111/ijsa.70022

Use:
- detail, clarity, structure;
- interview success;
- time to securing a position;
- compositional quality remains associated after controls.

Boundary:
- 183 students in a Canadian postsecondary co-op context;
- resume and cover letter quality were studied together;
- association with screening outcomes is not proof of job-performance validity;
- authors explicitly raise generative-AI implications.

Evidence class: longitudinal/field job-search study.

## S08 — Prehire work experience validity

**Van Iddekinge, Arnold, Frieder, & Roth (2019). _A meta-analysis of the criterion-related validity of prehire work experience_. Personnel Psychology, 72(4), 571–598.**

DOI: 10.1111/peps.12335

Use:
- challenges simplistic "more experience = better future performance";
- supports separating experience quantity from evidence quality.

Key reported corrected correlations:
- job performance: .06;
- training performance: .11;
- turnover: .00.

Evidence class: meta-analysis.

## S09 — Biodata validity

**Meta-analysis of biodata in employment settings: Providing clarity to criterion and construct-related validity estimates (2021/2022). Journal of Applied Psychology.**

DOI: 10.1037/apl0000964

Use:
- structured biodata can have meaningful validity;
- supports distinction between scientifically designed biodata assessment and ordinary resume review.

Evidence class: meta-analysis.

## S10 — Resume validity concern

**Zhang, Roth, Van Iddekinge, Chen, Andrekovich, & Harrison (2025). _Resumes for Selection: Ubiquitous in Use but Little Evidence of Criterion Validity_. Academy of Management Proceedings.**

DOI: 10.5465/AMPROC.2025.10899abstract

Use:
- direct challenge to assuming holistic resume review predicts performance;
- reports low point estimates in two samples.

Boundary:
- public source is conference proceedings / abstract;
- important but not final evidence for universal conclusions.

Evidence class: preliminary multi-study conference evidence.

## S11 — Algorithmic hiring fairness

**Fabris et al. (2025). _Fairness and Bias in Algorithmic Hiring: A Multidisciplinary Survey_. ACM Transactions on Intelligent Systems and Technology, 16(1).**

DOI: 10.1145/3696457

Use:
- socio-technical algorithmic hiring;
- fairness definitions;
- datasets;
- mitigation;
- legal and computer-science perspectives.

Evidence class: multidisciplinary survey + systematic components.

## S12 — Hiring discrimination meta-analysis

**The state of hiring discrimination: A meta-analysis of recent correspondence experiments (2022). European Economic Review.**

DOI: 10.1016/j.euroecorev.2022.104315

Use:
- demonstrates that resume/application screening is vulnerable to differential treatment based on applicant characteristics;
- fairness cannot be treated as a final formatting check.

Evidence class: meta-analysis.

## S13 — Gender hiring audits

**Schaerer et al. (2023). _On the trajectory of discrimination: A meta-analysis and forecasting survey capturing 44 years of field experiments on gender and hiring decisions_. Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 179, 104280.**

DOI: 10.1016/j.obhdp.2023.104280

Use:
- context dependence and temporal change in audit-study findings;
- warns against simplistic universal bias claims.

Evidence class: preregistered meta-analysis.

## S14 — 2026 large résumé audit (provisional)

Boundary:
- preprint status; do not promote to stable rule without publication/review status check.
