# Contradiction Registry v0.1

## K001 — Resume success vs resume validity

Observation A:
Resume content/composition can predict recruiter judgments, interviews and time-to-job.

Evidence:
- Cole et al. 2007
- Tsai et al. 2011
- Wingate et al. 2025

Observation B:
Holistic resume assessment may have weak criterion validity for future performance; common prehire experience measures are weak predictors.

Evidence:
- Zhang et al. 2025 preliminary
- Van Iddekinge et al. 2019

Resolution:
Not a true contradiction if outcomes are separated.

```text
SCREENING EFFECTIVENESS
≠
JOB-PERFORMANCE VALIDITY
```

Architecture consequence:
Maintain separate outcome taxonomy.

## K002 — Work experience is valued vs work experience predicts poorly

Observation A:
Recruiters use work-experience information and relevant experience predicts interviews in some studies.

Observation B:
Experience amount/duration has weak criterion validity for later performance.

Resolution:
Recruiter value and performance validity answer different questions.

Open question:
Which **features of experience** are diagnostically stronger than raw duration?

## K003 — Writing quality matters vs writing quality may be non-diagnostic

Observation A:
Writing quality predicts job-search success (Wingate 2025).

Observation B:
GenAI can improve writing at low cost; writing quality may no longer identify applicant writing skill/effort.

Resolution:
Writing quality remains a transmission advantage but its latent signal meaning is unstable.

Operational consequence:
Optimize clarity, but never claim polished writing proves capability.

## K004 — Richer applicant information vs inference validity

Observation A:
Video/social/digital formats expose more applicant information.

Observation B:
Apers & Derous 2017 found richer résumé formats did not improve personality-inference accuracy.
Mönke et al. 2024 found social-media assessment validity/reliability limitations.

Resolution:
Information quantity/richness does not guarantee diagnosticity.

## K005 — Creative design can differentiate vs formal layout can be preferred

Observation A:
Creative formatting may increase distinctiveness/salience.

Observation B:
Arnulf et al. 2010 found formal layouts were preferred over creative layouts.

Resolution:
Distinctiveness is not inherently positive.
Visual effects depend on role, conventions, reader, medium and parsing constraints.

Status:
Unresolved across modern contexts.

## K006 — Digital hiring as innovation vs digital hiring as unfair

Observation A:
Digital methods can signal innovation.

Observation B:
The same methods can reduce procedural-justice perceptions.

Evidence:
- Folger et al. 2022

Resolution:
A technology can generate competing signals simultaneously.

## K007 — AI reduces human bias vs AI reproduces bias

Claim A:
Algorithmic tools may standardize evaluation and reduce some human inconsistency.

Claim B:
Algorithmic systems may learn or reproduce historical / linguistic / structural bias.

Evidence:
- Fabris et al. 2025
- AI & Society 2025

Resolution:
No general "AI is fairer" or "AI is more biased" rule is justified.
Fairness must be evaluated system-by-system against explicit comparison baselines.

## K008 — Fit predicts outcomes vs fit judgments may be biased

Observation A:
Fit constructs relate to preentry/postentry outcomes.

Observation B:
Recruiter-perceived fit can incorporate subjective stereotypes or non-job-relevant cues.

Resolution:
Separate:
- construct-level fit;
- evidence-supported requirement match;
- recruiter-perceived fit;
- culture similarity / affinity.

## K009 — Tailoring is beneficial vs tailoring can become keyword imitation

Observation A:
Tailoring is associated with interview proportion in modern field data.

Observation B:
Over-tailoring can become superficial terminology matching, inflate claims, or reduce authenticity.

Resolution:
Define tailoring as **relevance-weighted evidence selection and terminology alignment**, not copying the JD.

Status:
Operational definition provisional.

## K010 — Fairness effects are real vs direction changes across time/context

Observation A:
Large correspondence-experiment meta-analysis shows hiring discrimination across many grounds.

Observation B:
Gender-focused meta-analysis shows time trends and job-type-specific direction changes.

Resolution:
Keep discrimination as a protected risk class while refusing universal direction/magnitude claims.
