# Atomic Claim Registry v0.1

Status labels:
- ACCEPTED = sufficiently supported for current architecture use;
- PROVISIONAL = useful but needs more evidence / boundary testing;
- CONTESTED = evidence conflicts or validity is uncertain;
- CONTEXTUAL = accepted only within explicit context.

## C001 — Recruitment and selection must be separated conceptually

**Claim:** Recruitment and personnel selection are related but distinct scientific domains that both matter to CVs.

Evidence:
- Breaugh 2013
- Potočnik et al. 2021
- Sackett et al. 2026

Status: ACCEPTED

Operational implication:
Use Recruitment + Selection / Staffing as umbrella; do not reduce CV science to selection validity alone.

## C002 — Work requirements have multiple information classes

**Claim:** Work analysis commonly distinguishes work activities, worker attributes, and work context.

Evidence:
- Sanchez & Levine 2012

Status: ACCEPTED

Operational implication:
Target-role modeling must not be raw keyword extraction from the JD.

## C003 — CV screening is a signaling context

**Claim:** Personnel selection can be modeled as information exchange under asymmetric information and partly conflicting incentives.

Evidence:
- Bangerter et al. 2012

Status: ACCEPTED as theoretical mechanism

Operational implication:
Model credibility, self-presentation, signal diagnosticity, and possible signal inflation.

## C004 — Resume information affects recruiter screening judgments

**Claim:** Resume content can change recruiter impressions, fit perceptions and hiring recommendations.

Evidence:
- Cole et al. 2007
- Tsai et al. 2011
- Chen et al. 2011
- Piopiunik et al. 2020

Status: ACCEPTED

Outcome:
screening judgments / interview invitations, not job performance.

## C005 — Perceived fit can mediate resume effects

**Claim:** Recruiter-perceived P–J fit can mediate relations between résumé information and hiring recommendations.

Evidence:
- Tsai et al. 2011

Status: CONTEXTUAL

Boundary:
Taiwan campus-recruitment field setting.

Operational implication:
Track perceived fit as human inference, not objective role match.

## C006 — Prehire work-experience quantity is a weak general predictor

**Claim:** Common amount/duration/type measures of prehire work experience are weak predictors of later performance/turnover.

Evidence:
- Van Iddekinge et al. 2019 meta-analysis

Status: ACCEPTED

Key estimates:
- performance ρ ≈ .06;
- training performance ρ ≈ .11;
- turnover ρ ≈ .00.

Operational implication:
Do not equate years of experience with evidence strength.

## C007 — Structured biodata can be predictive

**Claim:** Structured/scored biodata inventories can show meaningful criterion validity.

Evidence:
- biodata meta-analysis, DOI 10.1037/apl0000964

Status: ACCEPTED

Non-edge:
structured biodata validity ≠ ordinary résumé validity.

## C008 — Holistic resume criterion validity remains uncertain

**Claim:** There is insufficient basis to assume holistic resume assessment is a strong predictor of future job performance.

Evidence:
- Zhang et al. 2025 conference proceedings abstract
- indirect support from work-experience validity concerns

Status: CONTESTED / IMPORTANT UNCERTAINTY

Operational implication:
Never market the CV engine as predicting job performance.

## C009 — Some skill signals causally affect interview choices

**Claim:** Randomized signals of cognitive/social skills on résumés can affect HR managers' interview invitation choices.

Evidence:
- Piopiunik et al. 2020

Status: ACCEPTED

Boundary:
German HR-manager experiment; effects vary by applicant type and signal.

## C010 — Writing quality predicts proximal job-search outcomes in one modern field context

**Claim:** Greater detail, clarity and structure in resumes/cover letters predicted interview proportion and shorter job-search duration beyond several content controls.

Evidence:
- Wingate et al. 2025

Status: CONTEXTUAL / STRONG MODERN PRIMARY

Operational implication:
Treat composition quality as consequential for screening.

Non-edge:
composition quality ≠ worker quality.

## C011 — Tailoring may improve interview outcomes

**Claim:** More tailored application materials were associated with a higher proportion of interviews in Wingate et al. 2025.

Status: PROVISIONAL

Boundary:
tailoring did not significantly predict fewer days in search in the same study; overlap with writing-quality detail requires further separation.

## C012 — Layout can influence shortlisting

**Claim:** Graphical résumé layout can alter shortlisting probability.

Evidence:
- Arnulf et al. 2010

Status: CONTEXTUAL

Operational implication:
Document representation is causal/behaviorally consequential.

Non-edge:
one experiment ≠ universal template law.

## C013 — Recruiter attention differs across résumé regions

**Claim:** Review time and attention to sections such as Experience/Education can be associated with advancement decisions.

Evidence:
- Pina et al. 2023

Status: PROVISIONAL / CONTEXTUAL

Operational implication:
Human-readable hierarchy matters.

Do not promote:
"recruiters only read 6–7 seconds."

## C014 — Resume personality inference is generally inaccurate

**Claim:** Recruiters cannot reliably infer most Big Five traits from paper resumes, and richer video/audio formats do not solve this.

Evidence:
- Apers & Derous 2017

Status: ACCEPTED within studied construct

Operational implication:
Do not infer personality as factual candidate data from CV presentation.

## C015 — Impression-management language can change perceived fit

**Claim:** Some résumé/cover-letter self-presentation tactics can improve perceived fit/hireability judgments.

Evidence:
- Waung et al. 2017
- older supporting impression-management work

Status: CONTEXTUAL

Operational implication:
Separate persuasive effect from evidentiary strength.

## C016 — Algorithmic hiring is heterogeneous

**Claim:** Algorithmic hiring comprises multiple stages, systems, data types, metrics and mitigation methods; "ATS" is not one screening algorithm.

Evidence:
- Woods et al. 2020
- Fabris et al. 2025
- Sackett et al. 2026

Status: ACCEPTED

Operational implication:
Any machine-optimization claim must specify the assumed function/system.

## C017 — Hiring discrimination is empirically observable in application screening

**Claim:** Correspondence experiments show differential treatment of otherwise matched applications across multiple protected/salient characteristics.

Evidence:
- Lippens et al. 2022/2023 meta-analysis
- Schaerer et al. 2023 gender audit meta-analysis

Status: ACCEPTED

Boundary:
effect direction/magnitude vary across group, place, job type and time.

## C018 — Digital selection methods affect applicant reactions

**Claim:** Greater digitalization can alter employer attractiveness through competing signals such as innovativeness and procedural justice.

Evidence:
- Folger et al. 2022
- Woods et al. 2020
- broader applicant-reaction literature

Status: ACCEPTED for broader selection ecosystem

Operational implication:
Socio-technical systems are bidirectional: organizations also signal to applicants.

## C019 — GenAI changes what polished application writing can signal

**Claim:** When AI lowers the cost of producing polished/tailored application materials, writing quality may become less diagnostic of underlying writing skill or effort.

Evidence:
- Wingate et al. 2025 theoretical interpretation + field results

Status: PROVISIONAL / HIGH-PRIORITY

Operational implication:
The engine may optimize clarity while explicitly refusing to infer candidate capability from polish.

## C020 — LLM resume screening can reproduce bias

**Claim:** Some LLM-based screening setups can reflect gender-coded patterns from job descriptions and candidate attributes.

Evidence:
- AI & Society 2025 study DOI 10.1007/s00146-025-02564-8

Status: PROVISIONAL / SYSTEM-SPECIFIC

Operational implication:
LLM screening requires explicit fairness audits; do not assume AI neutralizes human bias.
