# Application Targeting Research v1.0

## Research question

Should a CV be personalized for one company, one role, one vacancy, or not personalized at all?

## Working conclusion

The strongest defensible unit is not:

```text
one company = one completely unique CV
```

It is:

```text
one evidence base
→ one target application
→ one application-specific CV variant
```

where:

```text
TARGET APPLICATION
=
company
× vacancy
× target role
× application context
```

This is a modeling rule, not a claim that every word must change.

## Evidence distinction

### A. Role/vacancy relevance — supported

Resume information affects recruiter judgments and perceived person–job fit.

Tsai et al. (2011) found resume content such as work experience and education influenced hiring recommendations through recruiter-perceived fit.

Source:
- DOI: 10.1111/j.1464-0597.2010.00434.x

Operational implication:
- candidate evidence should be selected and prioritized against the actual target requirements.

### B. Specific signals can change screening choices — supported/contextual

Piopiunik et al. (2020) experimentally manipulated skill signals in CVs and found some signals changed HR managers' interview invitation choices.

Source:
- DOI: 10.1016/j.euroecorev.2020.103374

Operational implication:
- what evidence is surfaced matters;
- signal usefulness depends on applicant/context;
- do not treat all keywords/signals as equally valuable.

### C. Tailoring is associated with interview success — supported association

Wingate et al. (2025) studied resume/cover-letter composition in a field sample.

Their tailoring scale ranged from:
- no tailoring;
- industry-focused;
- company-focused.

Tailoring correlated with interview proportion, but its incremental coefficient was not statistically significant after controls that included writing quality and candidate variables.

Source:
- DOI: 10.1111/ijsa.70022

Operational implication:
- do not claim that company-focused tailoring independently guarantees better outcomes;
- writing quality, clarity, structure, and relevant evidence remain important;
- company-specific tailoring should be treated as a contextual layer, not the foundation.

### D. Selection is a signaling environment — supported theory

Bangerter, Roulin, & König (2012) model personnel selection as a signaling game under information asymmetry.

Source:
- DOI: 10.1037/a0026078

Operational implication:
- tailoring changes which truthful signals are salient;
- it must not become signal fabrication.

## Evidence status

### ACCEPTED for runtime

1. Candidate evidence should be mapped to the target role/vacancy.
2. High-relevance supported evidence should receive greater salience.
3. Different vacancies at the same company can justify different CV variants.
4. Tailoring must preserve candidate truth and claim calibration.
5. Company context should only influence the CV when it materially changes relevance or interpretation.

### PROVISIONAL / CONTEXTUAL

1. Company-focused tailoring has an independent causal benefit beyond strong writing and relevant evidence.
2. Employer mission/values language improves screening outcomes.
3. A fully unique CV for every company is superior to a well-targeted vacancy-specific variant.

These are not runtime laws.
